# AI, Innovation, and Skills

## Purpose

This activity connects the Andrew Ng / Silicon Valley Girl interview to your own skills. Last time, many of the skills we named were soft skills: communication, teamwork, curiosity, leadership, and adaptability. Those matter. Today we will focus on hard skills: specific abilities you can learn, practice, demonstrate, and apply to real problems.

By the end of this activity, you should choose one hard skill you want to begin learning.

## 1. Watch For Skills

As you watch the interview, pay attention to what people are able to do with AI and what human skills still matter.

Discuss:

1. What did employees in the video build with AI?
2. What problem were they trying to solve?
3. How did AI help them move faster or do something they could not easily do before?
4. What human knowledge, judgment, or technical skill did they still need?
5. What risk, tradeoff, or opportunity did you notice?

## 2. Hard Skill Brainstorm

In your group, make the longest useful list you can of hard skills that could help someone innovate, build, analyze, operate, or improve something.

A skill only counts if your group can answer:

```text
What is the skill?
What can someone actually do with it?
Where might this skill be useful?
```

Hard skill examples:

- Spreadsheet modeling
- Data analysis
- SQL or database querying
- Python programming
- Web design or app development
- AI prompting and workflow automation
- Digital marketing analytics
- Financial analysis
- Accounting software
- CAD or 3D modeling
- Video editing or content production
- Customer discovery interviewing
- Survey design and analysis
- Project management software
- Equipment repair or maintenance
- Refrigeration systems
- Motors, pumps, or mechanical systems
- Basic electronics or sensors
- Process mapping
- Lean operations
- Sales pipeline management
- Market research

## 3. Hard Skill Hunt

Choose one of these options as a group:

1. Look at job descriptions for careers that interest you. What hard skills appear repeatedly?
2. Think about a business or industry you care about. What hard skills do people need to do real work there?
3. Think about your campus innovation project. What hard skill would help your group understand, test, or build something better?

Write down at least five hard skills you found:

Then discuss:

1. Which of these skills seems most valuable?
2. Which one seems most learnable in the next few weeks?
3. Which one could help with your project or future career?

## 5. Choose One Skill To Learn

Pick one hard skill from your top three. This is the skill you will begin learning and practicing.

Answer:

1. What exactly do I want to be able to do with this skill?
2. Why does this skill matter for my future, my track, or my project?
3. What resource will I use to begin learning it?
4. What small practice task can I complete this week?
5. How will I show evidence that I actually practiced?

## 6. Skill Learning Assignment

Before the next class, spend focused time beginning to learn the hard skill you selected.

Your skill can be connected to software, data, business, a trade, technology, operations, or how something works.

Examples:

- Learn basic SQL and write three simple queries
- Build a small spreadsheet model
- Analyze a small dataset and make one chart
- Create a simple landing page or mockup
- Learn how refrigeration works and explain the basic cycle
- Learn how an electric motor works and sketch the main parts
- Try an AI workflow for summarizing interviews or organizing research
- Learn the basics of a software tool used in a job you want
- Watch a technical tutorial and reproduce the result
- Read job descriptions and identify the hard skills you would need to qualify

Bring evidence of your learning. Evidence could include notes, screenshots, a small demo, a diagram, a file you created, a short explanation, or a list of what you tried and what confused you.
