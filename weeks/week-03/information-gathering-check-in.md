# Information Gathering Check-In

## Purpose

Your group has started gathering information about your project. Today you will compare what you found, think about different ways to gather better information, and leave with clear next steps.

By the end of this activity, your group should have:

- A clearer explanation of your problem
- A summary of the information you gathered
- Feedback from another group
- Better questions to investigate
- A specific next step before the next class

## 1. Ways To Gather Information

Different methods help you learn different things. As you work on your project, try to use more than one method.

### Observation

Watch what people actually do in the real setting.

Use this when you want to understand behavior, routines, friction, or workarounds.

### Interviews

Talk with people about their experiences, choices, frustrations, and needs.

Use this when you want to understand how people think and feel about the problem.

### Surveys

Ask several people the same short set of questions.

Use this when you want to look for patterns across a larger group.

### Existing Data

Look at schedules, policies, usage numbers, prices, records, reports, maps, or other available information.

Use this when you want evidence beyond personal opinion.

### Online Research

Look for examples, competitors, best practices, similar problems, or solutions people have tried elsewhere.

Use this when you want to understand what already exists.

### Small Experiments

Try a small version of an idea and see what happens.

Use this when you want to test an assumption before building something bigger.

### Expert Conversations

Talk to someone with field knowledge or responsibility for the area.

Use this when you need context, constraints, history, or practical advice.

## 2. Pair With Another Group

Pair your group with another group. Each group will share while the other group listens and asks questions.

## 3. Group Share

When it is your turn, explain:

```text
Our problem is:

The information we gathered is:

How we gathered it:

What surprised us:

What we still do not know:
```

## 4. Listening Group Questions

When another group shares, ask helpful questions. Your job is not to judge their idea. Your job is to help them think more clearly.

Ask:

1. What evidence do you have that this problem matters?
2. Who else should you talk to?
3. What information might be missing?
4. What assumption are you making that still needs to be tested?
5. What is one small next step you could take before the next class?

## 5. Switch Roles

After the first group shares and receives questions, switch roles. The second group shares, and the first group asks questions.

## 6. Decide Next Steps

After both groups have shared, return to your own group and write:

```text
One thing we learned from the other group:

One question we need to answer next:

One person we need to talk to:

One information-gathering method we will use next:

One action we will take before the next class:
```

## 7. Class Debrief

Be ready to share:

1. Which information-gathering method seems most useful for your project right now?
2. What is one question your group needs to answer next?
3. What is one action your group will take before the next class?

## Reminder

Do not gather information only to prove your first idea is right. Gather information to understand the problem, notice what you missed, and decide what to try next.
