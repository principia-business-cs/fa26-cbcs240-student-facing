## 1. Look up the book and read a synopsis — 10 minutes

Search for **Everyday Millionaires by Chris Hogan**. Read the [publisher’s synopsis on Apple Books](https://books.apple.com/us/book/everyday-millionaires/id1447672591) and the [Ramsey Solutions summary of its National Study of Millionaires](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/retirement/the-national-study-of-millionaires-research). No purchase is needed.

**Starting point:** The book argues that substantial wealth can be built through ordinary financial habits rather than requiring a large inheritance or an unusually high salary. It draws on a survey of more than 10,000 U.S. millionaires. The related study emphasizes consistent investing and reports that many respondents invested through workplace retirement plans. These are the authors’ claims to examine, not a guarantee of anyone’s future results.

Discuss and record:

1. In two or three sentences, what is the book’s main argument?
2. What did you learn that surprised you? Identify one specific finding and its source.
3. What is the difference between a high income and a high net worth? Use **net worth = assets − liabilities** to create a simple example.
4. Why might the habits described help someone accumulate wealth? Would they work today? What conditions or barriers could affect the outcome?
5. What can a survey of people who became millionaires tell us? What can it not tell us about people who followed similar habits but did not become millionaires?

**Record:** Main argument: __________  Evidence: __________  Limitation or question: __________

## 2. Investigate ABC Supply — 10 minutes

Read [ABC Supply’s history and milestones](https://www.abcsupply.com/about-us/our-history/history-and-milestones/). Search for an additional credible source if you need to investigate a claim further.

**Starting point:** Ken and Diane Hendricks founded ABC Supply in 1982 by acquiring three building supply centers. The company’s history describes a focus on serving contractors and treating them with respect. It expanded through acquisitions and new locations. This provides a case for examining innovation in service, distribution, and business operations.

Discuss and record:

1. What does the company sell, and who are its customers?
2. What customer problem or unmet need did its founders identify?
3. Why might their approach have worked? Support your explanation with one detail from the history.
4. Where do you see innovation: a new invention, better service, distribution, operations, or another approach? Explain your choice.
5. Could someone apply a similar approach today in a local business, such as HVAC, construction, or carpet installation? Give a concrete example and one obstacle.
6. How does building wealth through business ownership differ from building wealth through employment and long-term investing? Consider time, risk, capital, and responsibilities.

**Record:** Customer problem: __________  Business response: __________  Application today: __________

## 3. Find out what pass-through taxation means — 10 minutes

Search for **IRS pass-through taxation** and read the opening explanation on the [IRS S corporations page](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/s-corporations).

**Starting point:** Pass-through taxation generally means business income is reported by its owners for federal income-tax purposes. An S corporation passes income, losses, deductions, and credits through to shareholders. It generally avoids the corporate-level federal income tax that applies to C corporations, although exceptions and other taxes can apply. “Pass-through” does not mean “tax-free.” This is a tax treatment, not a separate tax called a “pass-through tax.”

Discuss and record:

1. Explain pass-through taxation to someone who has never taken a business class.
2. Who generally reports the income in an S corporation: the company or its owners?
3. Why would “the company paid no federal corporate income tax” be insufficient evidence that nobody paid tax on its profits?
4. How might owners’ tax obligations affect decisions to retain money in a business, distribute cash, or fund expansion?

**Practice case — fictional, not ABC Supply’s finances:**

A hypothetical S corporation has $100,000 of taxable profit. Alex owns 60% and Jordan owns 40% for the entire year. Assume income is allocated in proportion to ownership. For classroom arithmetic only, apply an illustrative 25% tax rate to each owner’s allocated profit, ignoring all other tax rules.

| Calculate | Alex | Jordan |
|---|---:|---:|
| Allocated taxable profit | $________ | $________ |

Would retaining cash in the company necessarily eliminate the owners’ tax obligation on allocated profit? Explain the difference between **income allocated for tax purposes** and **cash distributed to an owner**.

Do not infer ABC Supply’s current tax classification from this exercise. A claim about a real company’s tax treatment requires separate evidence.

Each group reports for up to two minutes. For more than six groups, assign each group one main topic and use one-minute reports. While listening, note one idea another group adds or challenges.

Whole-class prompts:
