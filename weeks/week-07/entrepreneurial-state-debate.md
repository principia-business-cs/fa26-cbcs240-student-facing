# The Entrepreneurial State Debate

## Purpose

Today we are comparing two different views of innovation:

- **The Entrepreneurial State** by Mariana Mazzucato
- **The Myth of the Entrepreneurial State** by Deirdre Nansen McCloskey and Alberto Mingardi

You researched your assigned book or side before class. In class, you will use what you found to explain the argument, listen to the other side, ask questions, and defend your group's position.

You may have used any useful source: book summaries, reviews, author interviews, videos, AI summaries, articles, publisher pages, or library sources. Your paragraph must include the source you used.

## Group Assignments

```text
Group 1: The Entrepreneurial State
Book: The Entrepreneurial State
Author: Mariana Mazzucato
Main position: Government can be an active driver of innovation, not just a background supporter.

Group 2: The Myth of the Entrepreneurial State
Book: The Myth of the Entrepreneurial State
Authors: Deirdre Nansen McCloskey and Alberto Mingardi
Main position: Innovation is mostly driven by markets, entrepreneurs, and decentralized discovery, not state planning.
```

## The Big Question

```text
Who drives innovation: government, entrepreneurs, markets, institutions, or some combination of all of them?
```

## Homework Paragraph

Before class, each student should research their group's book or side and write one paragraph.

Your paragraph should include:

1. Which book or side you researched
2. What you think the book is mainly about
3. Two or three main points from the book or argument
4. The source you used
5. One thing you found interesting, surprising, or questionable

Use this format:

```text
Name:

Book/side I researched:

Source I used:

Paragraph:
```

Your paragraph should be clear enough that someone who did not research your book can understand the argument.

## 50-Minute Class Plan

### 1. Individual Check-In: 5 Minutes

Review your homework paragraph. Underline or mark:

- Your clearest main point
- Your best evidence, example, or source detail
- One question you have for the other side

### 2. Meet With Your Group: 8 Minutes

Group 1: **The Entrepreneurial State**

Group 2: **The Myth of the Entrepreneurial State**

Each group should compare what members found.

As a group, prepare:

```text
Our side's main claim is:

Our strongest two or three points are:

The best source or example someone found is:

One question we want to ask the other side is:
```

### 3. Alternating Share: 12 Minutes

Groups alternate sharing main points.

Round 1:

- Group 1 shares one main point.
- Group 2 shares one main point.

Round 2:

- Group 1 shares another main point.
- Group 2 shares another main point.

Round 3:

- Group 1 shares what they think is the strongest evidence or example.
- Group 2 shares what they think is the strongest evidence or example.

As you listen, write down one question for the other side.

## Questions To Ask The Other Side

Use questions like:

1. What does your side think government does well in innovation?
2. What does your side think government does poorly?
3. What role do entrepreneurs play in your argument?
4. What role do markets, competition, and prices play?
5. What examples does your side use to support its argument?
6. What might your side be underestimating?
7. Where might both books agree?

## Question Round: 10 Minutes

Each side asks the other side questions.

Your goal is not only to win. Your goal is to understand the strongest version of the other argument.

When answering, use evidence from your homework research and group discussion.

## Defend Your Side: 10 Minutes

Each group prepares and gives a short defense.

Use this format:

```text
Our side's main claim is:

The best reason to believe this claim is:

An example or evidence we found is:

The biggest weakness or question for our side is:

Our response to that weakness is:
```

Each group gives a short defense to the class.

## Reminder

A good discussion does not require everyone to agree. It requires people to understand the arguments clearly, use evidence, ask honest questions, and notice tradeoffs.
