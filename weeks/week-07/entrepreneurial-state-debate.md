# The Entrepreneurial State Debate

## Purpose

Today we are comparing two different views of innovation:

- **The Entrepreneurial State** by Mariana Mazzucato
- **The Myth of the Entrepreneurial State** by Deirdre Nansen McCloskey and Alberto Mingardi

You do not need to read the full book during class. Your job is to quickly research the argument, understand the main points, explain what you found, and discuss which view is more convincing.

You may use any useful source: book summaries, reviews, author interviews, videos, AI summaries, articles, publisher pages, or library sources. Keep track of what source you used.

## The Big Question

```text
Who drives innovation: government, entrepreneurs, markets, institutions, or some combination of all of them?
```

## Individual Research And Paragraph

Each student should research one of the two books.

Write one paragraph that includes:

1. Which book or side you researched
2. What you think the book is mainly about
3. Two or three main points from the book or argument
4. The source you used
5. One thing you found interesting, surprising, or questionable

Use this format:

```text
Book/side I researched:

Source I used:

Paragraph:
```

Your paragraph should be clear enough that someone who did not research your book can understand the argument.

## 50-Minute Class Plan

### 1. Research And Write: 12 Minutes

Individually research your assigned book or side. Write your paragraph and include your source.

Focus on understanding the argument, not memorizing details.

### 2. Form Two Groups: 3 Minutes

Group A: **The Entrepreneurial State**

Group B: **The Myth of the Entrepreneurial State**

Each group should quickly compare what members found.

### 3. Alternating Share: 12 Minutes

Groups alternate sharing main points.

Round 1:

- Group A shares one main point.
- Group B shares one main point.

Round 2:

- Group A shares another main point.
- Group B shares another main point.

Round 3:

- Group A shares what they think is the strongest evidence or example.
- Group B shares what they think is the strongest evidence or example.

As you listen, write down one question for the other side.

## Questions To Ask The Other Side

Use questions like:

1. What does your side think government does well in innovation?
2. What does your side think government does poorly?
3. What role do entrepreneurs play in your argument?
4. What role do markets, competition, and prices play?
5. What examples does your side use to support its argument?
6. What might your side be underestimating?
7. Where might both books agree?

## Question Round: 10 Minutes

Each side asks the other side questions.

Your goal is not only to win. Your goal is to understand the strongest version of the other argument.

When answering, use evidence from what you researched.

## Defend Your Side: 8 Minutes

Each group prepares a short defense.

Use this format:

```text
Our side's main claim is:

The best reason to believe this claim is:

An example or evidence we found is:

The biggest weakness or question for our side is:

Our response to that weakness is:
```

Each group gives a short defense to the class.

## Final Turn-In

Submit your individual paragraph.

Your submission must include:

```text
Name:

Book/side researched:

Source used:

Paragraph:
```

## Closing Reflection

If time remains, answer:

```text
After hearing both sides, I think innovation is mostly driven by:

The strongest point I heard today was:

One question I still have is:
```

## Reminder

A good discussion does not require everyone to agree. It requires people to understand the arguments clearly, use evidence, ask honest questions, and notice tradeoffs.
