# Problem, Solutions, and Interviews

## Purpose

Your group has already noticed something around campus that could be improved. Now your job is to go deeper into the problem before falling in love with one solution.

By the end of this activity, your group should have:

- A clearer problem statement
- Five possible ways to address the problem
- A list of people to talk to
- Interview questions that will help you learn from those people

## 1. Get Clear About The Problem

Discuss these questions as a group:

1. What exactly is the problem we noticed?
2. Who experiences this problem most directly?
3. Why does this problem matter enough to improve?
4. What do people currently do to work around it?

Write your current problem statement:

```text
[Group of people] need a better way to [do something] because [why it matters].
```

## 4. Prepare Questions To Ask

Use these questions to learn from people. Your goal is not to prove that your idea is good. Your goal is to understand the problem more clearly.

1. Can you describe the last time you experienced or noticed this problem?
2. What makes this frustrating, inconvenient, stressful, or inefficient?
3. What do you currently do to deal with it?
4. If this problem were improved, what would be different for you?
5. Which of our possible solutions seems most useful, and why?
6. What concerns would you have about that solution?
7. Who else should we talk to before deciding what to test?

## 5. Before You Leave Class

- The people you plan to talk to

- The first solution your group might test
