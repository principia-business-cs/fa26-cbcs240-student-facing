# Group Worksheet: Problem and Goal

## Purpose

Your group has noticed something around campus that could be improved.

Your job today is to turn that observation into:

1. A clear problem statement
2. A clear goal statement

Do not rush to a final solution yet. First, understand what is happening, who is affected, and what better would look like.

## Part 1: Describe What You Noticed

Answer these questions together.

1. What exactly did your group notice?

2. Where did you notice it?

3. When does it happen?

4. Who is involved?

5. Is this something you saw once, or have you noticed a pattern?

## Part 2: Identify the People Affected

1. Who experiences this problem most directly?

2. Who else is affected indirectly?

3. Who currently has to deal with it, fix it, avoid it, or work around it?

4. Are different groups affected differently? For example: first-year students, commuters, athletes, faculty, staff, visitors.

## Part 3: Understand the Current Reality

1. What do people currently do when this happens?

2. What workaround do they use?

3. What makes the current situation frustrating, confusing, slow, expensive, stressful, unfair, or wasteful?

4. What might people have accepted as normal even though it could be improved?

## Part 4: Ask Better Questions

Write at least three **I wonder if...** questions.

Examples:

- I wonder if this happens more at certain times of day.
- I wonder if first-year students experience this differently.
- I wonder if the real issue is information, motivation, access, habit, design, or policy.
- I wonder if people already have a workaround.
- I wonder if technology would actually help.
- I wonder if a non-technology solution would work better.

Your questions:

1. I wonder if...

2. I wonder if...

3. I wonder if...

Now write at least two **Yes, and...** builds.

Examples:

- Yes, and this could also affect people who...
- Yes, and we could test that by...
- Yes, and another version of the goal might be...
- Yes, and the first small prototype could be...

Your builds:

1. Yes, and...

2. Yes, and...

## Part 5: Define the Problem

Before writing your final problem statement, answer:

1. What is the real problem underneath what you noticed?

2. Why does this problem matter?

3. What happens if nobody fixes it?

4. Is the problem mainly about information, access, time, cost, motivation, coordination, design, policy, habit, or something else?

5. Are you describing the problem, or are you already jumping to a solution?

## Part 6: State the Goal

Before writing your final goal statement, answer:

1. What would be better if this problem improved?

2. What should people be able to do more easily?

3. What should become faster, clearer, safer, cheaper, more welcoming, or less stressful?

4. What would count as progress?

5. How would you know the situation improved?

6. Can the goal stay open enough that more than one solution is possible?
