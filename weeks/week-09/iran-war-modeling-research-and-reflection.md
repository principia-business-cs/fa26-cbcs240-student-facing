# Iran War Modeling: Video Preparation and Reflection

## Before Our Next Class

Watch the full [assigned Robert Pape video on modeling the Iran war](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fhL44S4HfCk) before class. Come prepared to explain the argument, examine the model's assumptions, and discuss what you learned.

This connects to our course's emphasis on gathering evidence, making sense of uncertainty, considering risks and tradeoffs, and revising our thinking. A model is useful only if we understand what it assumes and how we could test it.

## Watch and Take Notes

Use these questions to guide your preparation. Brief notes are enough; you do not need to write a separate essay for each question.

1. **What are Pape's main arguments?** What does he think could happen in the Iran war?
2. **How does he support his arguments?** What evidence, historical examples, or reasoning does he use? Which do you find most convincing?
3. **How likely do you think the outcome he describes is to happen?** Why might it happen, and why might it not? Explain what would need to be true and what could change the outcome.
4. **What did you learn, and what do you still question?** Use another source to check one of his claims and bring one question for class discussion.

Record the video's publication date and at least one timestamp for a claim you plan to discuss. Separate what Pape says from your own interpretation.

## Find Another Source

Use at least one additional credible source to investigate a claim from the video. This may be an academic analysis, an interview with another expert, reliable reporting, or an official document.

- Who produced the source, and when?
- Does it support, complicate, or challenge Pape's argument?
- Is any disagreement about evidence, assumptions, goals, or predictions?

Save the source link and a specific finding. Distinguish what was known when the video was recorded from later developments. Evaluate an official statement as a statement from that actor, rather than automatically treating it as independent confirmation.

## Submit: Half-Page Reflection

**Before class, submit approximately half a page of double-spaced writing in Canvas.** Use a readable 12-point font and standard 1-inch margins; about 125–175 words is a useful guide. Put your name at the top and source links below the reflection; those do not count toward the half-page length.

In your own words, explain:
- One main idea from the video.
- What you learned by examining an assumption or checking a claim against another source.
- Your assessment and one question or uncertainty you want to discuss.

Use at least one specific example or piece of evidence. You may agree, disagree, or remain undecided; explain your reasoning. You may use AI to help locate sources or clarify concepts, but verify factual claims and write the reflection yourself.

## Come Prepared to Discuss

Bring your reflection, notes, video timestamp, additional source link, and one discussion question. Be ready to explain your findings without relying on someone else to summarize the video for you.

We will reflect individually, compare findings in pairs or small groups, and share with the class:
1. What is his main argument, and how does he support it?
2. How likely is the outcome he describes? Why might it happen, and why might it not?
3. What did your other source add, and what evidence would change your mind?

Focus on evidence, uncertainty, and the consequences of different choices. Listen carefully to classmates who reach different conclusions.
