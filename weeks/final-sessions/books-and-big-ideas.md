# Books and Big Ideas: Expanding How We Think

CBCS240: Technology and Innovation | Session 2 of our final three classes | 50 minutes

**Which book, interview, lecture, podcast, or topic was most interesting to you, and what did you learn that you did not know before?**

Today connects two course objectives: gaining a broader understanding and appreciation of intellectual activity, and learning to analyze and critically evaluate ideas, arguments, and points of view. Listening and discussing also give us practice working with others.

The aim is to explore how wider knowledge can help us notice problems, challenge assumptions, and imagine possibilities for innovation.

## 1. Choose one resource or topic | 0-5 minutes

Choose something you actually explored during the course:

- *Guns, Germs, and Steel*
- Jeffrey Sachs's lecture on geography and the wealth of nations
- *The Entrepreneurial State*
- *The Myth of the Entrepreneurial State*
- Modern Monetary Theory (MMT)
- Gary's Economics
- The retirement discussion featuring Teresa Ghilarducci
- Another book, interview, lecture, or topic we explored

It can be your favorite, the most surprising, or something you disagreed with. You do not need to have read the entire book. Identify the material you actually used: a book passage, video, lecture, podcast, or AI-assisted summary.

## 2. Reflect and revisit your source | 5-13 minutes

Use your notes or revisit the resource. Write brief answers:

1. What is one main idea, in your own words?
2. What did you learn that you did not know before? Why did it interest you?
3. What claim do you find convincing or questionable, and why? What evidence supports it, or would you need to check it?

Distinguish what the author or speaker argues from your own interpretation. If you used an AI summary, identify the tool and book, and a claim you would check against the original source.

## 3. Share in small groups | 13-28 minutes

Form groups of three or four. Give each person time to explain their chosen idea and what they learned. Divide the time evenly.

After each person shares, listeners ask:

- How does that connect to another idea or experience?
- What assumption or evidence should we examine?
- How could this idea change the way we understand a problem or opportunity?

You may disagree with an idea and still learn from examining it. Seek understanding before trying to persuade.

## 4. Bring the ideas together | 28-38 minutes

Each group shares one interesting connection and one unresolved question with the class. Divide the ten minutes evenly among groups.

Discuss:

- What did these different perspectives help us notice that we might otherwise overlook?
- How could one idea inform a specific innovation, decision, or experiment?
- What would we need to understand about the setting before applying it?

## 5. Write toward your final paper | 38-48 minutes

Write a paragraph you can revise for your three-page final:

> The resource or topic I chose was __________, which I explored through __________.
>
> One idea I took from it was __________.
>
> Before this, I thought or did not know __________.
>
> It broadened or challenged my thinking because __________.
>
> This connects to innovation because __________.
>
> One question or limitation I would still investigate is __________.

Use your own experience and reasoning. A thoughtful explanation of why your view stayed the same is also valid. Save a link or reference to your actual source.

## 6. Exit reflection | 48-50 minutes

What is one idea from a classmate that helped you think differently? What do you still need to clarify for your paper?

## If innovation talks need to begin today

The final class has room for eight five-minute talks. If more students need a turn, the instructor will reserve five minutes per additional speaker today and announce the order in advance.

A shortened version preserves this book discussion: choose and write for 5 minutes, discuss in groups for 10 minutes, share for 5 minutes, and draft for 5 minutes. This leaves 25 minutes for up to five innovation talks. Finish polishing the paragraph after class. For larger classes, the instructor will need to allocate additional speaking time across the three sessions.
