# Your Project's Next Experiment

CBCS240: Technology and Innovation | Session 2 of our final three classes | 50 minutes

Return to your campus innovation project. Your goal today is to use what you know to choose and improve one small next experiment. Bring any interview notes, observations, sketches, prototypes, or feedback you already have.

## 1. Take stock individually | 0-5 minutes

Write: What did we originally think the problem was? What do we now know? What important question remains unanswered?

## 2. Build an evidence map with your project group | 5-15 minutes

Create three columns:

| What we observed or heard | What we think it means | What remains uncertain |
|---|---|---|
| Include the source or situation. | Label interpretations as interpretations. | Identify something that could change your decision. |

Choose your strongest evidence. Does it support your original problem and goal, or suggest a revision?

## 3. Exchange questions with another group | 15-25 minutes

Take five minutes per project. Explain your problem, intended beneficiary, and evidence.

The other group asks:

1. What makes this problem worth addressing?
2. Which assumption could cause your solution to fail?
3. What is a simpler way to learn whether your approach helps?

Listen and record the questions before defending your idea.

## 4. Design the next experiment | 25-37 minutes

Complete:

> We believe __________.
>
> We will test this by __________ with __________.
>
> We will look for __________.
>
> If we observe __________, we will __________.

Specify the person to consult, a question to ask or prototype to try, and the evidence you need. Identify one cost, risk, or tradeoff.

Choose a test small enough to carry out in a future class period or short meeting. A conversation, paper prototype, comparison of existing alternatives, or simple measurement may be enough.

## 5. Rehearse and revise | 37-45 minutes

Let another group try your interview questions or walk through your prototype. Ask what was unclear and revise one part.

Classmate feedback is a rehearsal unless classmates are the actual intended users. Identify what you still need to learn from the people affected by the problem.

## 6. Reflect individually | 45-50 minutes

- What evidence changed or challenged your thinking?
- What would your next decision be, and why?
- How does this experience support or complicate your definition of innovation?

Keep your evidence map and experiment plan as material for your [final reflection paper](../../assignments/final-course-reflection.md). A completed product is not required for today's exercise.
