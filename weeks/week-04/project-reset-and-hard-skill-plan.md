# Project Reset and Hard Skill Plan

## Purpose

Some projects slow down because the group is not sure what to do next. That is normal. Innovation work often gets messy after the first idea. Today is a reset: your group will check what you know, name what is unclear, choose a sharper next step.

By the end of class, you should have:

- A clearer version of your campus issue
- One project direction for the next week
- Better questions to ask people
- One small action your group will take
- A personal plan for the hard skill you will learn and demo later

## 1. Quick Project Status Check

As a group, answer honestly:

```text
Our campus issue is:

The people most affected are:

The information we have gathered so far is:

The part that feels unclear or stuck is:

The next decision we need to make is:
```

## 2. Energy Check

Choose the sentence that best describes your project right now:

1. We know the problem and need to test a solution.
2. We have a problem, but we need better evidence.
3. We have too many possible directions.
4. We are not sure the problem matters enough.
5. We need to talk to the right people.
6. We need a smaller version of the project.

Then answer:

```text
Why did we choose that sentence?

What would help us move forward today?
```

## 3. Pair With Another Group

Pair with another group. Each group gets a few minutes to explain:

```text
Our issue is:

What we have learned so far:

What we are considering doing:

Where we feel stuck:
```

The listening group should ask questions, not give a speech.

Good questions:

1. Who is experiencing this problem most directly?
2. What evidence do you have that this is a real problem?
3. What assumption might you be making?
4. Who else should you talk to?
5. What is the smallest version of this idea you could test?
6. What would count as progress by next class?
7. What might make this solution hard to actually use?

## 4. Choose A Next Move

After hearing questions from another group, choose one next move.

Pick one:

- Interview three people affected by the issue
- Observe the problem in the real setting
- Make a quick survey with five or fewer questions
- Research how another school, business, or organization handles this issue
- Sketch or prototype one possible solution
- Test a tiny version of the idea
- Talk to someone with responsibility or expertise in the area
- Narrow the problem to a smaller group of people or a smaller situation

Write your next move:

```text
Our next move is:

We chose this because:

Who will do it:

When it will be done:

What evidence we will bring back:
```

## 5. Make The Project Smaller

If your project feels too big, shrink it.

Use this format:

```text
Instead of solving this for everyone:

We will focus on this group:

In this specific place or situation:

For this specific problem:
```

Example:

```text
Instead of solving campus transportation for everyone:

We will focus on students without cars:

When they need to get to Walmart or Alton:

For the specific problem of planning reliable rides.
```

## 6. Hard Skill Plan

You are also choosing one hard skill to learn over the next four weeks. A hard skill is something specific you can practice, demonstrate, and apply.

Examples:

- Spreadsheet modeling
- Data analysis
- SQL or database querying
- Python programming
- AI workflow automation
- Digital marketing analytics
- Financial analysis
- Accounting software
- CAD or 3D modeling
- Video editing
- Survey design and analysis
- Project management software
- Equipment repair or maintenance
- Refrigeration systems
- Motors, pumps, or mechanical systems
- Basic electronics or sensors
- Market research

Choose one hard skill and answer:

```text
The hard skill I want to learn is:

What I want to be able to do with this skill:

Why this skill matters for my future, my track, or my project:

The resource I will use to start learning:

What I will practice first:

What evidence I can show after four weeks:
```

## 7. Make A Four-Week Learning Plan

Keep the plan small enough that you can actually do it.

```text
Week 1: I will learn or practice:

Week 2: I will learn or practice:

Week 3: I will learn or practice:

Week 4: I will prepare to demo:
```

Demo evidence could include:

- A small file you created
- A screenshot
- A chart or spreadsheet
- A short working demo
- A diagram you can explain
- Notes from a tutorial you followed
- A before-and-after example
- A short explanation of how something works
- A list of what confused you and what you figured out
