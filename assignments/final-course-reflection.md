# Course Final: What Innovation Means to Me

CBCS240: Technology and Innovation

**The final assessment for this course is an individual three-page reflection paper.**

Reflect on what you learned, how you now define innovation, and the strengths and weaknesses of the course. Use specific experiences rather than a list of topics. You may draw on class discussions, your campus project, your completed hard-skill experience, and the closing class activities.

## Connecting theme: Expanding the ideas we use to understand problems

One theme of this course is that innovation benefits from an expansive understanding of the world. Encountering ideas from different fields can help us notice problems, question assumptions, see connections, and imagine alternatives. Understanding the field in which we hope to innovate helps us decide which ideas might apply and what we still need to investigate.

Our explorations included *Guns, Germs, and Steel*; Jeffrey Sachs's lecture on geography and the wealth of nations; *The Entrepreneurial State* and *The Myth of the Entrepreneurial State*; material on Modern Monetary Theory (MMT) and Gary's Economics; and a retirement discussion featuring Teresa Ghilarducci.

You encountered these perspectives through books, long-form interviews, lectures, podcasts, or AI-assisted book summaries. The purpose was to broaden the ideas available to you and examine them critically. Exposure to an idea does not require agreement with it.

## What to address

### 1. What did you learn?

Explain two or three ideas or experiences that changed, challenged, or strengthened your thinking.

- What did you believe or understand before?
- What experience or evidence affected your thinking?
- What do you understand now, including any uncertainty that remains?

**Within this section, choose one book, lecture, interview, podcast, or topic you explored in the course.** Briefly explain its main idea in your own words, then focus on your response:

- What surprised you, challenged an assumption, or connected to something you already knew?
- How did this idea broaden, challenge, or strengthen the way you understand a problem or opportunity?
- How might that wider understanding influence something you would investigate, design, or do differently?

Choose something you actually explored. You do not need to summarize every resource or read an entire book for this assignment. Identify how you encountered the idea: for example, a particular interview, lecture, podcast, or AI-generated summary. If you used a summary, be clear about that and acknowledge an important question you would need to verify before relying on it.

This connection belongs within the three-page essay; it is not an additional paper or a separate book report.

### 2. How do you define innovation?

Develop your own definition and explain it through an example.

- How does your definition distinguish innovation from an idea, an invention, or an improvement?
- What roles do understanding a field, learning from different perspectives, gathering evidence, experimenting, and adjusting play?
- When does a broad idea become useful for innovation? Explain how you would connect it to a specific context or test.
- How has your definition changed during the course, or why has it stayed the same?

You do not have to use the instructor's exact wording. Support your definition.

### 3. What will you carry forward?

Use your campus project or completed hard-skill learning experience to reflect on your own decisions.

- What did you try, and what did you learn from the result?
- What would you do differently?
- How could you apply the learning in your future work, community, or personal life?
- What risk, opportunity cost, or tradeoff would you consider?

An unfinished project or unsuccessful attempt can provide strong evidence of learning.

### 4. What were the course's strengths and weaknesses?

Identify at least one strength and one weakness, using specific examples.

- What helped you learn, and why?
- What limited your learning, and why?
- What concrete change would you recommend? How might it help, and what tradeoff could it create?

Your grade does not depend on praising the course or agreeing with the instructor. Thoughtful criticism and positive feedback are equally welcome.

These activities support the same three-page paper. They do not increase its length or require additional essays.

## Format and submission

- Three pages of essay text, double-spaced, using a readable 12-point font and one-inch margins.
- Write in the first person. Organize your ideas into connected paragraphs; headings are optional.
- Put your name and course at the top. A separate title page is unnecessary.
- Identify your chosen book or topic and include a link or reference to the material you actually used. For an AI summary, identify the tool and the book it summarized. Identify any additional sources as well. References do not count toward the three pages. New outside research is not required.
- Submit individually through the Canvas final-reflection assignment. See Canvas for the deadline and accepted file formats.

The final class includes time to develop and revise your reflection. Your paper should represent your own experiences and reasoning.

## How the paper will be evaluated

| Criterion | Weight | What strong work demonstrates |
|---|---:|---|
| Depth of reflection | 40% | Explains how and why thinking developed, considers uncertainty, and identifies meaningful future applications. |
| Specific examples and connections | 30% | Uses concrete course experiences and a thoughtful connection to the chosen book or topic to support claims and a reasoned definition of innovation. |
| Constructive course evaluation | 20% | Explains strengths and weaknesses with examples and offers a feasible improvement with a tradeoff. |
| Organization and clarity | 10% | Presents a clear, coherent essay that addresses the prompt and follows the format. |

Before submitting, check: Have I explained what I learned, reflected on one chosen book or topic, defined innovation, connected learning to my life, and evaluated the course honestly with examples?
